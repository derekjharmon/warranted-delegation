The model is fitted and `analysis/analysis.py` now writes Table 1 to `output/table1.md`. The results support your hypothesis: districts with more low-income students have a clearly smaller share of ninth graders above the national median, holding school resources constant.

**Model:** a fractional logit (Papke & Wooldridge 1996, already in your references). It's a logit model for an outcome that is a share, and it keeps predicted shares between 0 and 1. Each district counts equally, matching the 43.7% mean from the data step. Standard errors are HC1, p-values are exact and two-tailed, and the percentage variables stay on their 0–100 scale.

**Controls I chose:**
- **Student demographics:** % low-income, % Asian, % Black, % Hispanic.
- **School resources:** pupil–teacher ratio, per-pupil spending, average teacher salary, average teacher experience, % year-round schools, % charter schools.
- **Left out:**
  - PCTAF, the share taking UC/CSU college-prep courses. Course-taking is probably partly a result of math achievement, so controlling for it would hide part of the effect.
  - The premade interaction columns in the dataset.
  - PERMINTE, the % minority teachers. It describes who the teachers are rather than how much the district spends.

**Results (303 districts):**
- **Low-income share:** coefficient −0.0227 (SE 0.0020, p = 2.59e-30). Converted to a change in the share, each extra percentage point of low-income students goes with about 0.50 fewer percentage points of ninth graders above the median (SE 0.04, p = 2.52e-32). Moving from the 25th to the 75th percentile of LOWINC (about 29 points) means roughly 14 points fewer above the median.
- **Racial composition:** all three shares matter. The Asian share is positive; the Black and Hispanic shares are negative.
- **Spending, class size, salary and experience:** none is significant (p from 0.20 to 0.58). That fits Hanushek's (1997) finding of no consistent link between resources and achievement.
- **Year-round schools:** the only resource variable below .05, at p = 0.0429. It's one marginal result among six resource tests, so I'd describe it cautiously.
- **Fit:** R² = 0.80, measured as the squared correlation between observed and predicted shares. A fractional logit has no ordinary R², so the table note says how this one is calculated.

**Before you write this up:**
- **This is a cross-section of 303 districts in one year.** The low-income result is a strong association with resources held constant, not a causal effect. The memo should be worded that way, especially next to Krueger's experimental evidence.
- **Krueger's year looks wrong in `notes/references.md`.** It lists 1998, but QJE volume 114(2) is the 1999 issue. Please check it and fix the in-text citation to match.
