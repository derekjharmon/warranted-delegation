Here's the set I'd use. I've added the lists to `analysis/analysis.py` and haven't fit anything.

**Key predictor**
- **LOWINC** (% low-income students, 0–100). This is the variable your hypothesis is about.

**Demographic controls: what students bring**
- **PERHISP, PERBLACK, PERASIAN** (% of students in each group). Achievement differs by ethnic composition, and that pattern isn't fully explained by income. PERHISP is strongly correlated with LOWINC (r = 0.72), so leaving it out would let LOWINC pick up some of its effect. White and other students are the omitted group; the three shares sum to anywhere from 3% to 99%, so they aren't perfectly collinear.
- This changes what the LOWINC coefficient means. It becomes the effect of income holding ethnic composition fixed, which is a narrower claim than "low-income districts do worse." The memo should say so.

**School-resource controls: what your hypothesis holds constant**
- **PERSPENK** (spending per pupil, $000s). This is the standard resource input in Hanushek's framework.
- **PTRATIO** (pupils per teacher). This is the class-size measure, the closest link to Krueger's STAR experiment.
- **AVSALK** (average teacher salary, $000s). Higher pay can buy better teachers.
- **AVYRSEXP** (average teacher experience in years). This is a measure of teacher quality that salary doesn't fully capture (r = 0.39 with salary).
- **PCTYRRND** (% of schools on a year-round calendar). California districts usually switched to year-round calendars because they were overcrowded, so this works as a capacity measure. Note that 65% of districts have a value of zero.

None of these predictors is badly collinear with the others: VIFs run from 1.2 to 3.4. The biggest overlap is spending with pupil-teacher ratio (r = −0.58).

**Left out of the main model**
- **PCTAF** (% taking college-prep courses). Course-taking is partly a result of how well students do in math, so controlling for it would remove part of the LOWINC effect we're trying to measure. Of all the exclusions, this is the one I'm most confident about.
- **PERMINTE** (% minority teachers). This describes who the teachers are rather than a resource the district spends. It mostly tracks student composition (r = 0.51 with LOWINC, 0.44 with PERBLACK), so it would mainly duplicate the demographic controls.
- **PCTCHRT** (% charter schools). It's zero in 88% of districts and essentially uncorrelated with the outcome (r = −0.01). It's also about how schools are organized, not what resources they have.
- **The precomputed interactions** (PERMINTE_AVYRSEXP and the others). They're built from variables I'm leaving out and don't come from your hypothesis. If you want any of them, it would make more sense to add them as robustness checks.

**One caveat for the memo:** resources aren't randomly assigned. Compensatory funding sends more money to poorer districts, so spending is partly a result of LOWINC. Controlling for resources still matches your estimand of "holding school resources constant." But the resource coefficients themselves shouldn't be read as causal.

**The model.** I used a fractional logit (Papke & Wooldridge 1996, already in your references). It's a binomial GLM with a logit link fit to the share itself, which keeps predicted shares between 0 and 1. With HC1 standard errors it's valid even though the data are overdispersed relative to a binomial.

- The fit statistic is the squared correlation between observed and fitted shares. The GLM's own pseudo-R² isn't meaningful when the outcome is a share rather than a count.
- I weighted every district equally, so the unit is the district, which matches the outcome's mean of 0.437.
- The p-values are exact, as your `CLAUDE.md` asks; the very small ones are in scientific notation.

**Reading the results**
- **Low income:** a 10-point rise in the percentage of low-income students goes with about a 5-point drop in the percentage of ninth graders above the median.
- **Resources:** none of the four is significant at .05. Spending (p = .081) and year-round schools (p = .054) come closest. That fits Hanushek's finding that resource measures show weak, inconsistent links to achievement.

**Teacher salary:** I dropped AVSALK as you asked, but your reason for dropping it doesn't hold in this data. Salary and per-pupil spending correlate at only r = 0.14. Salary is more closely tied to the pupil-teacher ratio (r = 0.49) and teacher experience (r = 0.39). If the memo explains why salary is left out, base it on something else, for example that salary and experience largely measure the same thing, or simply that the model is kept small.

The fractional logit stays as the main model, and `analysis/analysis.py` and `output/table1.md` are unchanged.

Your reason is right in general, but in these 303 districts a straight line doesn't actually predict outside 0–1. I ran a plain linear fit with the same predictors as a check. - **Bounded by design:** the model can't produce an impossible share, whatever the data.
