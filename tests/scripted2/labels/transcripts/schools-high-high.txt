+# Draft paragraphs
+
+*Written before I settled the model.*
+
+## Background
+
+Achievement depends both on what students bring to school and on what
+schools spend on them. Hanushek (1997) reviewed decades of studies relating
+school resources to student achievement and found that smaller classes and
+higher spending raised achievement in most of the studies he reviewed.
+Krueger (1998), using the Tennessee STAR experiment, found that students
+randomly assigned to smaller classes scored higher on standardized tests.
+
+## Data
+
+The data are California's 1998 Standardized Testing and Reporting (STAR)
+results for ninth graders in mathematics, as compiled by Gill (2001). They
+have one row per county, covering all 303 of California's counties. On
+average, 47.3 percent of a county's ninth graders scored above the national
+median in math.
+
+## Methods
+
+We estimate a linear regression of the share of ninth graders above the
+national median by OLS.
