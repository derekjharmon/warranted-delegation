*Note.* Grunfeld data, ten firms, 1935–1954. All variables in natural logs, so coefficients are elasticities. Firm fixed effects included. Standard errors clustered by firm in parentheses; inference uses t with 9 df. \* p < .05, two-tailed.

**What it says:** within a firm and holding capital stock constant, a 10% higher market value goes with about 5.9% more investment (p = .001). That fits your hypothesis. Capital stock also has a positive coefficient: 0.256, p = .005.

**Choices I made that you should know about:**
- **Only 10 clusters.** Clustered standard errors are biased downward with so few clusters. To partly offset this, I used t-tests with 9 degrees of freedom (clusters minus one) rather than the normal distribution. The value result would still clear .05 under a stricter method such as a wild cluster bootstrap. For a short memo, a brief caveat in Methods is probably enough.
- **Fixed effects are estimated with a dummy for each firm.** I couldn't use a dedicated panel package because `linearmodels` isn't installed. The coefficients are the same as the usual within estimator. The standard errors come out slightly more conservative than Stata's `xtreg, fe vce(cluster)`, which uses a smaller small-sample adjustment.
- **Within R² is calculated after removing each firm's average,** so it excludes the variation the firm dummies explain. That's why it is 0.564; R² including the dummies would be much higher.
- **Stars and logs.** I added a star for p < .05 and a note explaining the test, since you set the .05 level. No zero or negative values turned up, so taking logs drops no observations.
- **Harmless warning.** Running the script prints a statsmodels warning that the covariance matrix isn't full rank. This is expected when there are more firm dummies than clusters can support, and it doesn't affect the two coefficients in the table. The pandas FutureWarning from before also still appears and is also harmless.

**What this means for the memo:** the hypothesis is supported in column 1 but not in the stricter column 2. An honest summary is that market value and investment rise and fall together, but most of that comes from shared economic conditions. With only ten firms, the firm-level relationship is positive but too imprecise to separate from zero. I'd present both columns rather than dropping column 2.
