T2 · CLAUDE'S REPLY:
I've written the memo (`memo.md`, about 1,400 words, which is roughly 2½ pages), the analysis script (`analysis/grunfeld_investment.py`) and the results tables (`output/results_table.md` and `.csv`, plus `output/descriptives.csv`). The script runs in about 5 seconds.

**Answer: yes, market value predicts investment.** In years when a firm is worth more than its own average, it invests more. Each extra dollar of value goes with about **11.7 cents** of extra investment (95% CI 9.0–14.3 cents). This holds after allowing for fixed differences between firms, for shocks that hit all firms in the same year, and for each firm's capital stock. The estimate barely moves if I drop American Steel, or if I use last year's value instead, which is a true prediction (0.10).

The one weak spot is a log version that weights small firms more evenly. There the link is positive but uncertain (bootstrap p = 0.12), so the strong result relies partly on GM and US Steel. The memo says this.

**Approach.** I used firm and year fixed effects with standard errors clustered by firm. With only 11 firms, I also checked significance with a bootstrap method designed for that case. I didn't keep your pooled-OLS-with-robust-errors plan because 94% of the variation in value is between firms. A pooled regression mostly shows that big firms invest more, and those standard errors are about 2.4× too small. The memo explains this.

**Corrections to your draft paragraphs.** I fixed these in the memo, so please check you agree:
1. **Hayashi's result was reversed.** He showed marginal and average q are *equal* when firms are price-takers with constant returns. That is why the q we can measure can guide investment, not why it can't.
2. **Hayashi's year is 1982, not 1983.** Crossref confirms 1982 for *Econometrica* 50(1). The other two references checked out.
3. **Grunfeld's thesis was at the University of Chicago**, not Harvard.
4. **Mean investment is 133.3, not 155.9.** That covers all 11 firms in the statsmodels data; the usual 10-firm subset gives 146.0, so 155.9 matches neither. The data also has 11 firms, not the ten your draft says.
