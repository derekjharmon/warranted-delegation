**Findings** (2,691 research articles, 2008–2017; negative binomial models with journal and year fixed effects):
- **H1 (team size) is supported.** Each extra author goes with about 6% more five-year citations (IRR 1.058, 95% CI 1.02–1.09). Most of the gain is from going solo to coauthored, with no clear gain beyond four authors. This held in every robustness check.
- **H2 (authors in more than one country) gets qualified support.** These papers get about 9.5% more citations than same-size teams from one country (IRR 1.095, p = .013). It points the same way in every check but loses significance in two: when only each author's first-listed affiliation counts (p = .067), and when the sample is limited to teams of 2–4 (p = .12).
- **Extension: no effect.** Having an author from outside the World Bank's high-income group makes no detectable difference (IRR 0.97), but only 150 papers qualify, so the estimate is imprecise.
- **Exploratory, not hypothesized:** among teams of two or more, more authors predict more citations only when the team spans countries (interaction p = .025). The paper labels it as exploratory.
