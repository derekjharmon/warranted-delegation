## 2. Country codes are two letters, not three
The docs describe `country_code` as "an ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 code (e.g. US)". The API agrees: authors and institutions carry codes like `US`, `GB`, `DE`. The notes say `USA`, `GBR`, `DEU`. The World Bank's income classification does use three-letter codes, so joining the two will need a crosswalk from two-letter to three-letter codes. A few non-standard cases need handling by hand, such as Kosovo (XK / XKX).

## 3. `counts_by_year` can't give a five-year window for 2008–2011 papers
- **Which years it covers:** the docs say "the last ten years… citations older than ten years excluded." In practice the API returned 2012 through 2026 (15 years). All 100 papers I sampled from 2008 start at 2012, so citations from 2008–2011 are simply absent.
- **What a year means:** it's the calendar year in which the citing work was published, not years since the paper came out. You'd have to compute the window as publication year to publication year + 4.
- **Early citations:** 53 citation-year entries in a sample of 100 papers from 2017 fall before 2017, likely from online-first or working-paper versions. You'll need a rule for those.
- **It's incomplete:** for 2008 papers, `cited_by_count` exceeds the sum of `counts_by_year` by a median of 45 citations. Those are the missing pre-2012 citations.

**A way around it:** filter the works that cite a paper (`filter=cites:W…`) and group them by `publication_year`. That returns the full history: for the top 2008 SMJ paper it gives 2008–2011 counts, and its 2012 and 2013 counts (85, 109) match `counts_by_year` exactly. The catch is one extra API call per paper, about 3,300 calls. The alternative is to move the sample to 2012–2021 and use `counts_by_year` as it is. I'd use the citing-works route and keep 2008–2017.
