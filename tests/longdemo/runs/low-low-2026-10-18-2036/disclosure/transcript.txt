**Decisions you should check:**

1. **Wrong ISSN for Organization Science.** 0170-8406, the ISSN in `notes.md`, is *Organization Studies*. I pulled Organization Science with its correct ISSN, 1047-7039. If you actually meant Organization Studies, tell me and I'll re-pull.
2. **Year means first appearance online.** OpenAlex's publication year is when a paper first appeared online, not its print issue year. As a result, a journal-year can mix two or three print volumes. AMJ 2012 and SMJ 2013 look inflated because both journals started posting papers online before print around then. It also shifts which papers fall inside 2008–2017 at the edges. Using that year as the start of the citation window is defensible. If you'd rather define the sample by print volume, that's a small change.
3. **Citations before 2012 come from a second query.** OpenAlex's `counts_by_year` only goes back to 2012, so for papers from 2008–2011 it can't build the window on its own. For the missing years I counted citing papers by their publication year, using a separate OpenAlex query. That query matched `counts_by_year` closely on the years I compared. Per your definition, the window covers the publication year plus the five following years, so it is six calendar years.
4. **The type filter wasn't enough.** Many non-research items in OpenAlex are labelled "article", so I added rules. Every dropped record is listed with its reason in `data/excluded.csv`.
   - **Japanese journal mixed in:** OpenAlex has merged the Japanese journal 組織科学 (*Soshiki Kagaku*) into Organization Science. Its papers are dropped because their DOIs belong to that journal or are missing.
   - **ASQ and AMJ short items:** anything of 11 pages or fewer, or with no page numbers, is dropped. In these two journals those were all book reviews, editors' essays or special-issue introductions.
   - **SMJ and Organization Science short items:** only items of 3 pages or fewer are dropped. This removes errata and editors' notes, but also a few short commentaries and replies. Longer commentaries and replies are still in the data.
   - **Duplicates:** where the same title appears twice in a journal, I kept one record. The other was an erratum or a second DOI for the same paper.
