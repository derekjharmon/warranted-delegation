T3 · CLAUDE'S REPLY:
The paper is finished: I cleaned the data, wrote the analysis plan before looking at any scores, ran the analysis, and wrote it up. `paper.md` is about 3,700 words with three tables and a figure, which should come to roughly 7–8 pages. Nothing is committed.

**Your notes need correcting in six places.** I checked them against the data and the registry entry (AEARCTR-0000714), and the paper uses the corrected versions:

1. **Task length:** the task ran for 10 minutes, not 15.
2. **Gift size:** the gift was 40 cents, not $1. Every finisher in that arm has a payout of 0.40.
3. **Score cutoff:** the registration drops scores of 4,000 or more. It mentions 3,500 only as what the pilot showed is physically possible. I used 4,000 and ran your 3,500 as a robustness check; nothing changes.
4. **Repeat workers:** not every row is a different worker. 196 workers started more than once, some in different arms. The registration says to drop them, so I did.
5. **Number of arms:** there are 18, not 16 as in your draft methods paragraph.
6. **Gneezy & Rustichini:** the paper is from 2000, not 2001. It also found the opposite of what your notes say: a small payment *lowered* performance compared with paying nothing. The paper now uses that as a contrast, since here even a tiny piece rate raised effort.

**The "task significance" arm doesn't test what Grant (2008) tested.** It just says "please try as hard as you can" and never mentions anyone being helped. The paper keeps the original label but describes it as a request for effort.

**Sample:** 10,111 workers (542–580 per arm). That is 250 more than the 9,861 the original authors report. They probably screened further using the per-second press data, which this file doesn't include. I didn't adjust my rules to match their number; the paper says this.

**One rule wasn't in the plan:** 466 sessions marked finished had no score recorded. I dropped them, and the paper reports it.

**Results:**

| Hypothesis | Result | Supported? |
|:-|:-|:-|
| H1: pay raises effort | 1 cent per 100 points: +507 points (0.70 SD). 4 cents: another +101. 10 cents: +43 more, not significant. | Yes, with sharply diminishing returns |
| H2: free motivators help, but less than the smallest piece rate | Social comparison +336, ranking +230, task significance +213. Each is well below the 1-cent rate (Holm-adjusted p < .001). | Yes, for all three |
| H3: a gift raises effort | 40 cents: +64 points (95% CI −20 to 149) | No |

- **Gift:** the confidence interval is too wide to rule out a small effect. It is clearly the worst value per point of any arm.
- **Social comparison:** it did about as well as the very-low-pay arm (1 cent per 1,000 points).
- **Exploratory findings:** these are labelled as unplanned in the paper. Bonuses for reaching 2,000 points matched the 10-cent rate at under a third of the cost. Paying the Red Cross instead of the worker did less, and raising the donation tenfold changed nothing.
- **Robustness:** completion didn't differ by arm (p = .42). The conclusions hold under all four planned checks: the 3,500 cutoff, keeping repeat workers' first session, approved workers only, and rank-based tests.

- **Published numbers:** I didn't quote the original paper's arm means, so please check our means against them.
