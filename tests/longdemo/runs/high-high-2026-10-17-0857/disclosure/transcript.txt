I've checked notes.md against OpenAlex. The collection is running in the background and I'll check its output when it finishes. Here's what I found in the notes.

## Notes claims that look wrong

1. **The Organization Science ISSN is wrong.** 0170-8406 belongs to *Organization Studies* (I looked it up in the OpenAlex sources API). *Organization Science* is 1047-7039 (print) / 1526-5455 (online). I'm collecting *Organization Science*, assuming the journal name is what you meant and not the number.
2. **`counts_by_year` can't build a five-year window for 2008–2011 papers.** The docs say it covers "the last ten years." In the data it actually starts at 2012 for every work, including 2008 papers. So the first five years are partly or entirely missing for 2008–2011 papers. Instead I count each paper's citing works by the year they were published (`filter=cites:<id>&group_by=publication_year`). I tested this on a 2008 paper: it gives 2008–2011 citations (2, 10, 21, 48) that `counts_by_year` doesn't have.
3. **Country codes are two letters, not three.** The docs say ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, e.g. `US`, and the records match. The World Bank's income-group file uses three-letter codes (`USA`), so joining the two needs a crosswalk table.
4. **OpenAlex didn't replace Google Scholar.** Google Scholar never offered bulk access, so there was nothing to close. OpenAlex was built as a successor to Microsoft Academic Graph after Microsoft retired it in 2021. This comes from my own knowledge, not the docs: the pages I fetched don't say where OpenAlex came from either way.

Not about the data source, but I noticed these too:
- The draft methods paragraph uses a three-year window, while the notes use five.
- Wuchty, Jones & Uzzi was published in 2007, not 2005 (*Science* 316(5827) is a 2007 issue).
- Freeman & Huang (2014) found the opposite of what the notes say: papers by ethnically *diverse* teams got *more* citations. That's from my own knowledge, so check it against the paper.

## Collection choices

- **Years: 2008–2017**, as in the notes. The 2017 papers' five-year window ends in 2021 or 2022, which is complete. I'm saving every year of citations, not just five, so you can still choose three versus five years, and whether the publication year counts as year 1.
- **Journals:** matched by OpenAlex source ID, on any location rather than only the primary one. Matching on any location adds 16 works whose primary record is a repository copy. They might be duplicates, so I'll check them by DOI.
- **Document types:** I'm downloading all 3,656 records, but only articles and reviews (about 3,266) get citation-year counts. Editorials, paratext (OpenAlex's term for front matter such as tables of contents), book reviews, errata and retractions are out because they aren't research papers. There are only 4 "reviews," and in these journals that label is probably just an article. One thing to expect: OpenAlex's "article" type will still include some editors' pieces (such as AMJ's "From the Editors"), so those will need a title-based screen.
- **Fields:** I'm keeping the full record rather than choosing fields now, because it costs nothing extra. The ones the analysis needs:
  - ID, DOI, title, publication year and date, journal, volume/issue
  - `type`, `is_retracted`, `cited_by_count`
  - `authorships`: author IDs, order, institutions with their country codes, `countries`, and the raw affiliation text, for papers where institution matching failed
  - `countries_distinct_count`

## How it's running

The script is `scripts/collect_openalex.py`, and output goes to `data/raw/openalex/` (`works.jsonl`, `citing_years.jsonl`, `manifest.json`). It uses the `OPENALEX_API_KEY` in your environment, because the keyless daily limit was already used up. I didn't take up the API's suggestion to request a key with your email address. The run needs about 3,300 calls, and the key has about 8,800 left today. The first run failed on a dropped connection, so I made the script retry and use smaller pages, and restarted it.

Nothing is committed, since commits are blocked in this project's settings.

## What the notes get wrong about the data

1. **One ISSN is for a different journal.** 0170-8406 is *Organization Studies*. *Organization Science* is 1047-7039. I collected *Organization Science*, assuming the name is what you meant.
2. **`counts_by_year` can't give a five-year window for 2008–2011 papers.** The docs say it covers the last ten years. In the data it starts at 2012 for every paper, so most or all of an early paper's first five years are missing. Instead I counted each paper's citing works by the year they were published, which goes back to the publication year.
3. **Country codes are two letters, not three.** The docs say ISO 3166-1 alpha-2 (`US`, `GB`), and the data match. The World Bank's income groups use three-letter codes (`USA`), so the join needs a code conversion table.
4. **OpenAlex didn't replace Google Scholar.** It succeeded Microsoft Academic Graph, which Microsoft retired in 2021. Google Scholar never offered bulk access to close. The help pages I fetched don't cover this; it's from my own knowledge.

Other things I noticed in the notes:
- The draft methods paragraph uses a three-year window; the rest of the notes use five.
- Wuchty, Jones & Uzzi was published in 2007, not 2005.
- I believe Freeman & Huang (2014) found that ethnically *diverse* teams were cited *more*, the opposite of what the notes say. That's from memory, so check it against the paper.

- **Years: 2008–2017**, as in the notes. Even the 2017 papers have a complete five-year window. I kept every citation year, so the window length (three or five years, and whether the publication year counts) can still be decided later.
- **Journals:** matched by OpenAlex's journal ID on any of a work's locations, not only the main one. That pulls in 16 records whose main copy sits in a repository.
- **Document types:** I kept every type in `works.jsonl` (3,656 records). Citation-year counts were collected only for `article` and `review` (3,273 papers). Editorials, front matter, book reviews, errata, retractions, preprints and conference abstracts are excluded because they aren't research papers.

I've estimated H1 and H2 on the complete-case sample (N = 2,768). **H1 holds across every check. H2 is borderline:** the multi-country estimate is about +7% throughout, but its 95% interval includes 1 in the main model. All tables and the figure are written by code to `results/stage4/`.

## Specification

The main model is a negative binomial of five-year citations (years Y+1 to Y+5) with **journal-by-year fixed effects** and robust standard errors:

- **H1:** citations on the log of author count. I report the effect of doubling team size, because each extra author plausibly adds less. On BIC this fits better than a straight line in author count. Category dummies fit slightly better on AIC but worse on BIC, so I show them alongside.
- **H2:** citations on a multi-country indicator, controlling for team size with **categories (1, 2, 3, 4, 5+)**. "Beyond team size" means the team-size control has to be flexible: multi-country teams are larger, so a rigid control could leave size effects in the multi-country term.
- **Journal-by-year fixed effects** rather than separate journal and year effects. Citation levels and the online-first shifts (AMJ 2012, SMJ 2013) differ by journal and year. There are 40 cells averaging about 70 papers each.
- **Negative binomial, not Poisson:** the data are heavily overdispersed (dispersion α ≈ 0.51). Poisson is shown as a robustness check.

## Could author count or multi-country be standing in for something else? (Table 3)

I added possible confounders one at a time, then all together:
- paper length;
- number of references;
- any US-based author;
- several institutions;

**H1 barely moves:** from 1.139 to between 1.121 and 1.150, and stays significant in every row.

**H2 doesn't shrink; it slightly grows:** 1.083 (p = 0.034) with all controls together. So multi-country status isn't standing in for these factors.

**H1** stays between 1.12 and 1.16 per doubling with p < 0.001 under:

- capping citations at the 99th percentile;
- Poisson instead of negative binomial;
- separate journal and year effects;
- standard errors clustered by journal-year.

## Every decision, with its reason

1. **Sample: complete case, N = 2,768.** Your decision: one common sample for H1 and H2.
2. **Outcome: five-year citation count, Y+1 to Y+5.** Accepted in Stage 1.
3. **Negative binomial.** Citation counts are heavily overdispersed. Poisson is a check.
4. **Journal-by-year fixed effects.** Citation levels and online-first timing vary by journal and year. Separate effects are a check.
5. **Robust standard errors.** Papers are separate observations with uneven variance. Clustering by journal-year is a check.
6. **H1 uses log author count, reported as the effect of doubling.** It allows each extra author to add less, fits better on BIC, and limits the pull of the 17-author paper. The straight line, categories and team-versus-solo are shown too.
7. **H2 controls team size by categories.** "Beyond team size" needs a flexible control.
8. **Multi-country means two or more distinct countries across all authors' affiliations.** That includes a solo author affiliated in two countries. A variant counting only differences between authors gives 1.068.
9. **Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macao coded separately from China in the main model.** That's how OpenAlex codes them. Merging them into China is a check.
10. **Possible confounders are checks, not main-model controls.** Some, such as references and length, may be results of team size, and controlling for them could absorb part of the real effect.
11. **Prior output is counted within the sample only.** That's the data I have. It's cut off at 2008, so early years understate it; the year effects partly absorb this.
12. **"Several institutions" counts distinct institution IDs.** Authors with no matched institution aren't counted, so this is a minimum.
13. **Self-citations removed on a random subsample of 1,200 papers.** The API budget covered about 1,400 calls today; the full sample needs 2,768. A citation counts as a self-citation if the citing work lists any of the paper's authors by OpenAlex ID. 49 papers have an author without an ID, so their self-citations are slightly undercounted. A full run is possible after the budget resets.
14. **Outliers: capping at the 99th percentile, plus dropping the large-team papers.** Dropping highly cited papers would select on the outcome, so I capped instead.
15. **Journal-specific effects: separate per-journal models plus a test of equal slopes in the pooled model.** That shows heterogeneity without splitting the main estimate four ways.
16. **Figure:** panel A shows the team-size categories against solo papers; panel B shows both main estimates across the checks on a log ratio scale. Colours are the dataviz skill's reference palette blue and orange, which passed the colour-blindness and contrast validator. It's a static PNG/SVG for a paper; every plotted number is in `figure1_data.csv`.
17. **No correction for multiple checks.** The checks show how stable the estimates are, not separate hypothesis tests. That's why I call H2 borderline rather than significant.
