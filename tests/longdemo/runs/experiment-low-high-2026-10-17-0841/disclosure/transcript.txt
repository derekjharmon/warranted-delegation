## 2. The notes against the data, paper and registration

**Sources:**
- **Paper:** the NBER working paper w22193, downloaded and read in full. I didn't get the published REStud version.
- **Registration:** the AEA registry page AEARCTR-0000714. I only saw it through a summarising fetch tool, but the exclusion rules it gave match what the paper quotes word for word from the registration.

### Treatment code table: partly checked, and one entry is wrong
The paper and registration list the arms in words, not as codes like "1.1". I couldn't find the replication package online, so the code-to-arm mapping isn't confirmed from the authors' files. Instead I checked it against how each code actually paid out.

**Confirmed by the payouts:**
- **1.3:** payout always 0.
- **1.1, 1.4, 1.2:** payout is exactly whole hundreds of points × 1¢, 4¢ and 10¢.
- **2:** whole thousands of points × 1¢.
- **5.1 / 5.2:** 40¢ if 2,000 points or more, otherwise 0.
- **5.3:** the same at 80¢.
- **6.1:** about 1% of workers got large payouts ($16–$28).
- **6.2:** about half got 2¢ per 100 points, the rest 0.

**Wrong:** code 10 (gift exchange) paid a flat **$0.40** to all 589 workers who have a payout. The paper ("a bonus of 40 cents") and the registration agree. The notes' "unexpected $1 bonus" is incorrect.

**Can't be told apart from the data:**
- 1.1 vs 3.1 vs 4.1 vs 4.2: all pay 1¢ per 100. The Red Cross arms record the payout as if paid to the worker.
- 1.2 vs 3.2: both 10¢ per 100.
- 5.1 (gain) vs 5.2 (loss): identical payouts.
- 1.3 vs 7 vs 8 vs 9: all pay nothing.

For those groups the labels rest on the notes alone.

### "16 arms, ~550 each": wrong count, right size
- **Arms:** the paper, the registration and the data all have **18** arms. The notes' own intro also says 18. (The registration text once says "seventeen", but it lists 18.)
- **Size:** "about 550 per treatment" is the paper's figure after cleaning (final N = 9,861). Raw counts are about 680–695 per code, so ~550 only holds after exclusions.

### The 3,500-point rule: misstated
The registration excludes subjects who **"score 4000 or more points"**. The 3,500 figure is only the reason given: the pilot showed scoring above 3,500 by hand is "physically impossible", so those subjects are probably using bots. The paper says it dropped 48 workers "above 4,000". In this file the two cutoffs differ: 65 rows are above 3,500 versus 50 above 4,000.

## 3. Other things the paper says that matter for cleaning
- **Registered exclusions:** the paper lists three:
  - not completing within 30 minutes (1,543 dropped);
  - re-entering as a new subject (364 dropped);
  - 4,000 points or more (48 dropped).
- **Exclusions added after registration:**
  - 721 workers lost to a Qualtrics server glitch that broke the score counter;
  - 187 workers whose work wasn't approved;
  - 114 workers with zero presses.
- **Can't identify the glitch rows:** there's no flag in this file, and the paper gives no time window. I can't reproduce that exclusion without the replication package.

  - The task lasted **10 minutes**, not 15.
  - Arm 9, "task significance", actually reads "We are interested in how fast people choose to press digits and we would like you to do your very best." That's a weak match for Grant's idea of task significance, which matters for how you frame H2.
  - Gneezy & Rustichini, QJE 115(3), was published in **2000**, not 2001.

This is not the published REStud version, so page numbers and some wording may differ there. Pages are given as printed page (PDF page). Two quirks come from the extraction: "˜300" is how the PDF's tilde came through, and "roles" is the paper's own wording.

## (a) Task length (10 minutes, not 15)

**§1 Introduction, p. 1 (PDF p. 3):**
> "The task for the subjects is to alternately press the "a" and "b" buttons on their keyboards as quickly as possible for ten minutes."

**§3.1 Real-Effort Experiment, p. 12 (PDF p. 14):**
> "The task involved alternating presses of 'a' and 'b' for 10 minutes, achieving a point for each a-b alternation"

**§3.1, p. 13 (PDF p. 15), quoting the instructions workers saw:**
> "The object of this task is to alternately press the 'a' and 'b' buttons on your keyboard as quickly as possible for 10 minutes."

## (b) Gift exchange bonus (40 cents, not $1)

**§2 Treatments and Model, p. 8 (PDF p. 10):**
> "The final social preference treatment is a gift exchange treatment modelled upon Gneezy and List (2009): "In appreciation to you for performing this task, you will be paid a bonus of 40 cents. Your score will not affect your payment in any way." In this treatment there is no piece rate"

**§4.1 Average Effort, p. 18 (PDF p. 20):**
> "subjects receive an unexpected bonus of 40 cents, unconditional on performance."

The same instruction text appears in Table 4, p. 52 (PDF p. 54).

## (c) Task significance instructions

**§2, p. 11 (PDF p. 13):**
> "The final manipulation is based on the influential literature in psychology on task significance (Grant, 2008): workers work harder when they are informed about the significance of their job. Within our setting, we inform people that "Your score will not affect your payment in any way. We are interested in how fast people choose to press digits and we would like you to do your very best. ## (d) Exclusion rules and counts

**§3.1, p. 14 (PDF p. 16), the registered rules:**
> "We also pre-specified the roles for inclusion in the sample. **Same page, the 721 glitch drop:**
> "The initial sample consists of 12,838 MTurk workers who started our experimental task. Of these, 721 were dropped because of a technical problem with the survey over a several-hour period when the software program Qualtrics moved to a new server. This sample exclusion, which we could not have anticipated, does not appear in the registration."

**pp. 14–15 (PDF pp. 16–17), the 48, 1,543 and 364 drops:**
> "We then applied the three specified sample restrictions. **p. 15 (PDF p. 17), the 187 and 114 drops and the final 9,861:**
> "Two additional restrictions were added: we dropped 187 workers because their HIT was not approved for some reason (e.g. they did not have a valid MTurk ID) as well as 114 workers who never did a single button press. > "Summary Statistics. The final sample includes 9,861 subjects, about 550 per treatment."

**Footnote 14, p. 15:**

## Order of the exclusions

The paper gives them in this order:
1. Qualtrics glitch: 721
2. The three registered rules, in the order "(i) … (ii) … (iii)":
   - above 4,000 points: 48
   - didn't finish: 1,543
   - logged in again: 364
3. "Two additional restrictions":
   - not approved: 187
   - no button presses: 114

**How certain the order is:** the paper never says the drops were made one after another. But 12,838 − 721 − 48 − 1,543 − 364 − 187 − 114 = 9,861 exactly. So each count is the number dropped at its step, after the earlier steps, and the steps don't overlap. That fits a sequential order but doesn't prove the one listed, and the order changes the counts.

**Two wording differences:**
- **4,000 cutoff:** the registration says "4000 or more"; the paper's description says "above 4,000". In our data nobody scored exactly 4,000, so this doesn't change anything here.
- **The registered "didn't finish" rule:** it's defined as not completing "within 30 minutes of starting". The paper's description of that step is "failing to complete the experiment".
