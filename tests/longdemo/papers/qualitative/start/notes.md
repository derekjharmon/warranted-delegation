# Notes: raising safety concerns in the Space Shuttle program

## The idea

The two Shuttle accidents are classic cases in organization studies of how
organizations handle warning signs: Challenger in 1986 and Columbia in 2003.
We know a lot about the launch decisions themselves. We know much less about
how the engineers and managers who lived through the program talk about
raising, escalating, and settling technical concerns day to day, and about
what each accident changed in that work. NASA's STS Recordation Oral History
Project interviewed many of them near the end of the program, and the
transcripts are public.

Research question: How did engineers and managers in the Shuttle program
raise and handle technical safety concerns, and how did the two accidents
change those practices?

An inductive study, in the Gioia tradition: a data structure built from the
transcripts, then a grounded model. About 8–10 pages.

## Data

Candidate interviews, from NASA's STS Recordation Oral History Project. Pick
six to eight, covering both accidents and both engineers and managers. Each
transcript is a verbatim record of the interview, so quotes can be used
exactly as they appear.

## Method

The Gioia approach, as I remember it: start from the aggregate dimensions the
literature gives you (voice, normalization of deviance, learning), sort the
informants' quotes under them into second-order themes, and then name the
first-order concepts within each theme. Show the result as a data structure
figure, with representative quotes in a table.

## Draft methods paragraph (from an earlier version)

We conducted ten semi-structured interviews with Shuttle engineers and
managers between 2010 and 2012, recorded and transcribed them, and coded
them following Gioia, Corley, and Hamilton (2013).

## Background and literature

- Vaughan (1996): Challenger was the product of amoral calculation. Managers
  knew the O-ring risk and knowingly broke safety rules to keep the launch
  schedule.
- Edmondson (2001): psychological safety, the shared belief that a team is
  safe for interpersonal risk taking, makes people willing to speak up.
- Columbia was lost in 2003 when a piece of ice from the External Tank struck
  the left wing during launch.
